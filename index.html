02 / Core concepts

Sepsis is more than an infection.

Sepsis is life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection. The immune system may fail to contain the pathogen while simultaneously injuring the host's own tissues.

01 — HOST RESPONSE

Dysregulated immunity

Innate and adaptive immune pathways become unbalanced. Inflammation, coagulation, endothelial activation and immune suppression can occur together.

02 — MDR PATHOGENS

Limited treatment options

Multidrug-resistant bacteria are non-susceptible to multiple antimicrobial classes, making timely effective therapy more difficult.

03 — ORGAN INJURY

Systemic consequences

Microcirculatory dysfunction, vascular leakage, thrombosis and cellular/metabolic changes can contribute to kidney, lung, brain and cardiovascular dysfunction.

03 / Pathophysiology

How the immune response unfolds

A simplified pathway. In real sepsis, these events overlap and vary by pathogen, infection source, host factors and time.

1. RecognitionPRRs detect microbial PAMPs and tissue-injury DAMPs.
2. ActivationMacrophages, monocytes, neutrophils and endothelium activate signalling pathways.
3. Mediator releaseTNF, IL-1, IL-6, chemokines and other mediators are released.
4. DysregulationEndothelial injury, coagulation changes, capillary leak and immune dysfunction develop.
5. Organ dysfunctionImpaired perfusion and cellular injury may cause multi-organ dysfunction.

Hyperinflammation

Excessive or poorly controlled inflammatory signalling can amplify vascular and tissue injury. “Cytokine storm” is a descriptive term, not a standalone diagnostic test.

Immunosuppression

Sepsis can also cause lymphocyte apoptosis, reduced antigen presentation, T-cell exhaustion and increased susceptibility to secondary infections.

Simultaneous states

Pro-inflammatory and anti-inflammatory processes can coexist. A patient may show inflammation in one pathway and immune paralysis in another.

04 / Interactive glossary

Understand the language of sepsis.

Search the terms or open a card for a concise definition. Common abbreviations are expanded on first use.

TNF-α MEDIATOR

Tumour necrosis factor alpha. A pro-inflammatory cytokine produced mainly by activated macrophages/monocytes and other cells. It promotes endothelial activation, fever and inflammatory signalling; excessive activity can contribute to shock and tissue injury.

IL-7 MEDIATOR

Interleukin-7. A cytokine essential for T-lymphocyte development, survival and homeostasis. It is being investigated as an immunostimulatory strategy in selected sepsis-associated lymphopenia; it is not routine standard therapy for all patients.

IFN / Interferons MEDIATOR

Interferons. A family of cytokines (including type I interferons such as IFN-α/β and type II IFN-γ) that regulate antiviral defence and immune-cell activity. Their effects in bacterial sepsis are complex and context-dependent.

Cytokine MEDIATOR

A small signalling protein released by immune and other cells. Cytokines coordinate inflammation, immune-cell recruitment, activation, growth and regulation. Examples include TNF-α, IL-1, IL-6, IL-10 and interferons.

Cytokine storm MEDIATOR

A term describing an excessive, dysregulated systemic inflammatory response with high levels or activity of inflammatory mediators. It may contribute to endothelial damage, vascular leakage, coagulation abnormalities and organ dysfunction. It is not synonymous with every case of sepsis.

PAMPs RECOGNITION

Pathogen-associated molecular patterns. Conserved microbial structures recognised by innate immune receptors. Examples include lipopolysaccharide (LPS), peptidoglycan, bacterial lipoproteins and flagellin. “PUMPs” may be a typo for PAMPs; confirm if you meant another term.

DAMPs RECOGNITION

Damage-associated molecular patterns. Endogenous molecules released or exposed by injured or dying cells, such as extracellular ATP, HMGB1 and mitochondrial components. They can activate innate immune pathways even without direct microbial recognition.

PRRs RECOGNITION

Pattern-recognition receptors. Innate immune receptors that detect PAMPs and DAMPs. Major groups include Toll-like receptors, NOD-like receptors, RIG-I-like receptors and C-type lectin receptors.

Toll-like receptors (TLRs) RECOGNITION

A family of PRRs located on the cell surface or in intracellular compartments. For example, TLR4 recognises LPS from many Gram-negative bacteria; TLR2 recognises several bacterial lipoproteins. Activation triggers intracellular inflammatory signalling.

Receptor RECOGNITION

A protein or molecular structure that specifically recognises a signal (such as a cytokine, hormone or microbial pattern) and initiates a cellular response. Receptors may be on the cell membrane or inside the cell.

Macrophage IMMUNE CELL

An innate immune cell that engulfs microbes and debris, produces cytokines, presents antigens and helps coordinate inflammation and tissue repair.

Neutrophil IMMUNE CELL

A rapidly recruited white blood cell that kills microbes through phagocytosis, granule enzymes, reactive oxygen species and neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs). Dysregulated activation can damage host tissues.

Immunoparalysis / immune suppression IMMUNE CONCEPT

A sepsis-associated state of impaired immune function, which may include reduced monocyte HLA-DR expression, lymphocyte loss and weaker responses to pathogens. It can coexist with ongoing inflammation.

MDR bacteria CLINICAL

Multidrug-resistant bacteria. Bacteria that are non-susceptible to at least one agent in three or more antimicrobial categories (a widely used international definition). Resistance patterns must be determined by laboratory susceptibility testing.

Sepsis CLINICAL

Life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection (Sepsis-3 definition). It is a medical emergency requiring prompt recognition and treatment.

Septic shock CLINICAL

A subset of sepsis with profound circulatory, cellular and metabolic abnormalities. Clinically, it is associated with a greater risk of mortality and is defined using vasopressor requirement and lactate criteria after adequate fluid resuscitation.

Immunomodulation CLINICAL

An approach intended to alter immune activity. Depending on the patient's immune phenotype and clinical context, strategies may aim to reduce harmful inflammation or restore impaired immune function. No single immune modulator is appropriate for every patient.

Healthcare-associated infections

A 2023 point-prevalence survey in 26 acute-care hospitals examined 8,076 patients; 2.4% had a healthcare-associated infection. This is an HAI statistic, not a sepsis rate.

Antimicrobial resistance

In that survey, resistance to third-generation cephalosporins was the most frequently observed resistance among microorganisms isolated from HAI cases (47.6%).

Surveillance matters

WHO's CAESAR network provides standardized antimicrobial resistance surveillance. AMR data can inform the MDR context but cannot be substituted for sepsis incidence.

06 / Treatment landscape

Can we modulate the immune response?

The central challenge is choosing the right intervention for the right patient at the right time. Immune-modulating therapies remain an active research area; they do not replace standard sepsis care.

FOUNDATION OF CARE

Control the infection

Prompt appropriate antimicrobials, cultures when feasible without delaying treatment, source control, and organ support are central to sepsis management. MDR risk requires local epidemiology and susceptibility results.

IMMUNE RESTORATION

IL-7 and related approaches

IL-7 may support T-cell survival and expansion in selected lymphopenic patients. Other investigational approaches include interferon-γ and strategies targeting immune-cell dysfunction. Clinical use is not universal standard care.

INFLAMMATION CONTROL

Targeted anti-inflammatory strategies

Potential approaches include blocking selected inflammatory mediators or pathways. Broad suppression may impair pathogen clearance, so patient selection and timing are critical.

Educational content only. Treatment decisions must be made by qualified clinicians using current guidelines, microbiology results and the patient's clinical condition.
